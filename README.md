# Computational-PolII-Termination-Mapping
Statistical annotation and machine-learning prediction of RNA Polymerase II termination regions based on T4ph mNET-seq signal

Project overview

Transcription termination is an essential step in the RNA Polymerase II (Pol II) transcription cycle that defines the downstream boundary of transcription units and enables proper release of engaged polymerase from DNA.

Although termination is closely connected with 3′-end processing and polyadenylation, Pol II release occurs across extended genomic intervals rather than at a single nucleotide position. Therefore, defining termination regions requires approaches capable of capturing broad signal patterns while preserving genomic resolution.

T4ph mNET-seq provides a strand-specific view of actively engaged Pol II carrying Thr4-phosphorylated CTD, a polymerase state associated with transcription termination. However, T4ph mNET-seq termination-associated regions are often broad, heterogeneous, and contain local fluctuations in signal intensity. Conventional peak-calling approaches designed for sharp enrichment peaks may fragment continuous termination regions or define boundaries based on local signal variation rather than the overall extent of the termination-associated interval.

This project develops a computational framework for identifying continuous Pol II termination-associated regions from T4ph mNET-seq profiles while maintaining the positional resolution of the original signal and reducing artificial fragmentation caused by local signal variability.

1.  Statistical termination caller

The statistical caller identifies high-confidence termination-associated regions directly from T4ph mNET-seq signal profiles:

50 bp binned T4ph mNET-seq signal
          |
          v
Gradient-based boundary detection
          |
          v
Gaussian mixture modelling of signal distributions
          |
          v
Signal-supported interval merging
          |
          v
Boundary refinement and filtering
          |
          v
Final termination regions


2. Machine-learning prediction model

The machine-learning component extends termination region identification beyond statistically detected examples.
The model learns T4ph mNET-seq signal patterns associated with termination regions and enables prediction of termination-associated genomic intervals in unseen samples.

T4ph mNET-seq 50 bp bins
          |
          v
Statistical termination annotations
          |
          v
Feature extraction
          |
          v
Universal XGBoost classifier
          |
          v
Genome-wide prediction
          |
          v
Termination-associated regions


Input data

T4ph mNET-seq signal

Required format:

chrom    start    end    signal

Signal is provided as 50 bp genomic bins.


Termination regions

BED format:

chrom    start    end

Generated by the statistical termination caller and used as training annotations.
